## Statute text (verbatim)

> §11-102 Procedures for conducting
> elections by mail. (a) Ballot packages for elections by mail shall
> include:
>
> (1) An official ballot;
>
> (2) A return identification envelope with postage
> prepaid;
>
> (3) A secrecy envelope or secrecy sleeve; and
>
> (4) Instructions.
>
> (b) To the extent practicable, the clerk shall
> mail a ballot package by non-forwardable mail to each registered voter in the
> county so as to enable voters to receive the ballot package at least eighteen
> days before the election; provided that the State and counties shall not be
> liable if the ballot package is received fewer than eighteen days before the
> election. The clerk shall continue mailing ballot packages to voters who register to vote no later than ten days before the date of
> the election and to voters who update their voter registration address
> no later than seven days before the date of the election and who have not yet voted;
> provided that the clerk may continue mailing ballot packages beyond the
> deadlines established by this subsection if the clerk determines that there is
> reasonable time for a voter to receive and submit the ballot package before the
> election. In determining the initial mailing date of the ballot
> packages, the clerk shall consider the mailing place of origin and the most
> recent postal service delivery standards. The clerk shall not mail a ballot
> package to any voter in the county register who is identified as having an
> outdated or non-deliverable mailing address. Nothing in this part shall be
> construed to change the responsibilities of the clerk or chief election officer
> under chapter 15 with respect to voters requesting to
> vote by absentee ballot or chapter 15D with respect to uniform military
> and overseas voters.
>
> (c) The exterior of the envelope containing
> the ballot package shall include instructions on how to obtain language
> translation services in Hawaiian and, at a minimum, the five most utilized
> foreign languages by limited English proficient persons in the State.
>
> (d) The clerks shall determine and provide for
> voter service centers and places of deposit pursuant to this part and section
> 11-92.1. [L 2019, c 136, pt of §2; am L 2021, c 213, §18; am L 2022, c 166, §2]

## What it means operationally

- **A ballot is mailed to *registered* voters only.** §11-102(b) speaks of "each registered voter
  in the county." Nothing in this section mails a ballot to someone not on the rolls. This is the
  single most misunderstood fact about Hawaiʻi's all-mail system. See
  [[mail-ballot-registration-cutoff]].
- **Two different cutoffs, two different populations.** Register by **10 days before** the
  election and the clerk keeps mailing you a package. *Update your address* by **7 days before**
  and the clerk keeps mailing. These are separate rules that get conflated constantly.
- **"and who have not yet voted"** qualifies the address-update branch — a voter who has already
  returned a ballot does not get a second package.
- **The 18-day target is a target, not a right.** The statute expressly disclaims State and county
  liability if the package arrives later than 18 days out.
- **The "reasonable time" escape hatch is discretionary and must never be promised.** The clerk
  *may* keep mailing past the cutoffs. For voter-facing copy, treat the deadline as hard — a
  "maybe" from a clerk is not something a campaign can put in a text blast.
- **Bad addresses are dropped, not forwarded.** Mail is *non-forwardable*, and the clerk is
  affirmatively barred from mailing to any voter flagged with an outdated or non-deliverable
  address. A voter who moved and never updated does not get a ballot at their new place *or* their
  old one. This is why the 7-day address-update deadline is a live GOTV concern, not a technicality.
- **Language access.** The envelope exterior must carry translation instructions in Hawaiian plus
  the five most-used LEP languages statewide — §11-102(c). Relevant if we ever assess whether a
  county's mailing complied.
- **Voter service centers and drop boxes are the county clerks' to establish** — §11-102(d),
  pointing to [[hrs-11-92.1]]. That is the path that stays open after the mail cutoffs.

## What the amendments did

- **Act 136 (2019)** — original enactment; the act that moved Hawaiʻi to all-mail elections.
- **Act 213 (2021), §18** — amended.
- **Act 166 (2022), §2** — amended. Per the statutory note, *"The 2022 amendment applies to all
  elections beginning with the 2024 primary election."* So the text above is the version in force
  for 2026.
