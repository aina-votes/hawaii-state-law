# County clerks / County Elections Divisions

**Layer: county.** Hawaiʻi has four counties — Honolulu, Hawaiʻi, Maui, Kauaʻi. Election
*administration* for mail voting is county work even though election *law* is state law. When a
question is "who actually does this," the answer is usually the clerk, not the State.

> Status is `derived` because this page is assembled from duties named in a single statute plus the
> OoE site. The clerks' full statutory role has not been traced. Do not treat this page as a
> complete account of their authority.

## Duties established in [[hrs-11-102]]

- Mail a ballot package, **non-forwardable**, to each registered voter in the county, timed so
  voters receive it at least 18 days before the election — §11-102(b).
- Continue mailing to voters who register ≤10 days out and who update their address ≤7 days out —
  §11-102(b). See [[mail-ballot-registration-cutoff]].
- **Not** mail to any voter flagged with an outdated or non-deliverable address — §11-102(b).
- Determine the initial mailing date with reference to the mailing place of origin and current
  postal delivery standards — §11-102(b).
- Exercise discretion to keep mailing past the deadlines where there is "reasonable time" —
  §11-102(b). Discretionary; never rely on it.
- **Determine and provide for voter service centers and places of deposit** — §11-102(d),
  cross-referencing [[hrs-11-92.1]].

## Practical notes

- Paper voter registration applications are submitted **to the County Elections Division**, per the
  Office of Elections. Returned ballots are also received by the Division — by 7:00 p.m. election
  day.
- Voter service centers are "established and operated by the County Elections Divisions" (OoE),
  which is why VSC locations and hours vary island to island and must be checked per county rather
  than assumed statewide.
