# Mail-ballot registration cutoff (the 10-day / 7-day rule)

## The rule

Hawaiʻi is an all-mail state, but **a ballot packet only reaches someone already on the rolls.**
Under [[hrs-11-102|HRS §11-102(b)]] the county clerk continues mailing ballot packages to:

| Voter | Must act no later than | Consequence of missing it |
|---|---|---|
| Someone registering to vote | **10 days before** the election | No ballot is mailed. They must vote in person. |
| Someone updating their registration address | **7 days before** the election (and who has not yet voted) | No ballot is mailed to the new address, and none is forwarded to the old one — mail is non-forwardable and the clerk is barred from mailing to a flagged bad address. |

Past those points the clerk *may* keep mailing "if the clerk determines that there is reasonable
time." That is discretionary. **Never promise it.** Treat the cutoff as closed.

## Why this is a copy-risk page, not a trivia page

After the cutoff, any GOTV text blast, phonebank script, carousel, mailer, or email that says
*"watch your mailbox"* or *"fill out the ballot you got"* is **wrong for the unregistered and the
recently-moved**, and it costs votes by sending those people to wait for something that will never
arrive. After the cutoff the ask changes to: **go to a Voter Service Center.**

Check today's date against [[deadlines]] before drafting any voter-facing GOTV copy.

## The trap in the State's own presentation

The Office of Elections labels the 10-day date the **"Paper Registration Deadline"** and, directly
underneath, states **"Voters may register online at any time or in-person at a voter service
center."** Both statements are true. Together they mislead.

- Online registration really is open at any time.
- **Registering online after the cutoff still does not get a ballot mailed.** The cutoff in
  §11-102(b) is about when the *clerk stops mailing*, not about which form you used. The word
  "Paper" in the OoE label describes the submission channel for that particular deadline, and
  readers reasonably but wrongly infer that the online channel escapes the mail cutoff.

Anyone reading the OoE sidebar quickly comes away believing late online registrants get a ballot.
They do not. They vote in person.

## The path that stays open

Same-day registration at a **Voter Service Center**: register at the door and vote the same visit,
any day the VSC is open, through 7:00 p.m. on election day. VSCs are established by the county
clerks under [[hrs-11-102|§11-102(d)]] and [[hrs-11-92.1]]; same-day registration authority is
[[hrs-11-15.2]] *(statute not yet ingested — the rule here is currently sourced to the OoE, not
to the statute text)*.

Ballots must be **received** by the county elections division by 7:00 p.m. on election day.
A postmark never counts.
